AI goes wherever the output is easiest to verify. Which means, adoption is coming in waves: coding was first, support was second, and GTM is next.

Wave 1: Coding
Code either runs or it doesn't, so you know in seconds. a16z called coding "an order-of-magnitude outlier" among enterprise AI use cases. 
(Examples: Cursor, Claude Code, Codex, et al)

Wave 2: Support 
A ticket gets resolved or it doesn't, so you know in minutes. Companies in this space grew fast because support has clear outcomes and a lot of repetitive work.
(Examples: Fin, Decagon, Sierra) 

Wave 3: GTM 
This one is trickier. Because it's more art than science. A deal takes a quarter to close, and there's no "correct" email (it's more subjective than code). That's why sales and marketing are behind.
(Example(s): the only real example at scale here, so far, is Clay—at $7.1B & ~$200M ARR. 
Other challengers are companies like Rox, Monaco, etc. But it's still so early for GTM tech.)

Navin Chaddha (Mayfield) recently said coding's scale is "30 to 50x" anything else, and "it has to happen in sales."

1/ I tracked 719 GTM podcast episodes over the last 24 months. 'Claude' went from showing up in 10% of episodes to 53%. 'Agents' went from 15% to 39%. (Hilariously... the biggest drop was 'quota' - going from 29% to 7%.)

2/ Yesterday, I asked on LinkedIn for people's favorite GTM tech, and the top answers were Claude, Grok Bot and Codex, ahead of any GTM point tool.

So...
GTM teams are borrowing the coding wave's tools to start their own. 
But... 
I suspect GTM technologies are going to have standalone solutions over time. The wave just hasn't fully hit yet. 

Each wave is held back by how fast you can tell whether the AI did a good job. 
Seconds for code, minutes for support, quarters for a deal. 
Whoever shortens that GTM loop will wins the next wave.

2027 is gonna be wylin in GTM tech land.
